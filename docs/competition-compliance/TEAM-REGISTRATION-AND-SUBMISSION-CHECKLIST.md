Complete this from the official registration dashboard, not from memory. The
[official 2026 event page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/) states a team
may have up to six people; confirm the current rules and local deadlines again.

member or attribute work to someone who did not do it.

## Submission content checklist

- [ ] Exact challenge selected; solution explains why it addresses that challenge.
- [ ] Problem, user, and mission relevance are understandable in the first minute.
- [ ] Working demo link or reproducible instructions are available.
- [ ] All code/assets/data sources and licences are cited; required attribution is visible.
- [ ] AI use is disclosed accurately; final generator/tool and final music rights are filled in.
- [ ] No NASA logo, insignia, endorsement, clinical-validation, or fabricated-data claim.
- [ ] Every simulated/demo value visibly says simulation/demo/model.
- [ ] Demo includes limitations: webcam is not VOR; OSI is a proposed prototype.
- [ ] Video has captions and any required accessibility material.
- [ ] Final readme includes setup, sources, licence, team roles, and reproducible event-time history.

## Final 30-minute gate

1. Open every link in the submission as an anonymous viewer.
2. Run the final demo once from the same release/commit you submit.
3. Check citations, licence notices, image/video/audio rights, and AI disclosure.
4. Make sure the submission text does not say “NASA approved,” “clinical,” “real
   VOR,” or “built in 48 hours” unless each is demonstrably true and permitted.
5. Take a PDF/screenshot of the completed submission confirmation.
